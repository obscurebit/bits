## 📖 Today's Bit

### Discontinued Brown

TASK 412 — REMOVE & REPLACE MILE 9.3 SB — 1.5 HRS
The scraper slipped. Paint flakes rained onto Marge's steel-toes, brown over brown, the federally mandated shade oxidizing to something like dried blood. The aluminum blank underneath was pitted where the old reflective sheeting had breathed. She wiped sweat with a glove cuff, smearing mineral spirits across her forehead. The halogen lamps in the drying bay hummed, warming the converted garage to ninety degrees. Sawhorses held three fresh blanks curing, their edges curled like bacon. Mineral spirits and cold asphalt — the shop's perfume. She checked her watch. Ten-fifteen. The rent was due at the township office by four.

TASK 412A — DISPOSE OF NON-COMPLIANT SHEETING — 0.25 HRS
She bundled the curled brown strips, adhesive yellowed to amber, and tossed them in the dumpster behind the salt barn. Earl, the night watchman, watched from his lawn chair. "Historical Society picking those up?" he called. His radio crackled a Tigers game.
"Routine maintenance," Marge said. "Line item 412."
"Uh-huh." He scratched his belly through a stained tee. "Cash on the side still green?"
"Green's the only color that spends." She didn't look at him. The historical society paid forty bucks a decal, cash, no invoice. The township paid her twelve-fifty an hour to pretend the old route never existed. The brown signs — HISTORIC ROUTE, white lettering on federally mandated brown — marked a highway the county commissioners voted to erase in '68. The road stayed. The signs didn't. Until the historical society found grant money and decided the erased highway was heritage. Now Marge applied decals to blanks the township bought, billed the hours to routine maintenance, and pocketed the difference. Forty dollars a sign. She needed twelve signs to cover rent and the electric bill. She'd done four this month. Eight to go.

TASK 412B — LOG BENCHMARK LOCATIONS — 0.5 HRS
The rodman's retirement file sat in her bottom drawer, folded into thirds. Henry Kettering. Thirty-two years county roads. He'd placed the benchmarks himself, brass caps driven into bedrock at mile markers 9.3, 14.7, 22.1. "Nobody uses benchmarks anymore," he'd told her, signing his pension papers. "GPS. Satellites. But the brass stays." He'd drawn a map on the back of a requisition form. X marks. Not on any township plat. Marge filed the map with the retirement paperwork. The trustees didn't ask. The historical society didn't know. She knew. That was the gate she kept.

She pulled the work order clipboard from the sawhorse. Ballpoint pressed hard. REMOVED NON-COMPLIANT SHEETING. INSTALLED BLANK PER MUTCD SEC. 2D.38. She wrote it neat, the way her mother taught her: printing like typewriter keys, each letter a small act of order. Her mother. Who called every Sunday. *Did you eat? Did you pay the electric? Did you call Dr. Fenwick about your knee?* Logistics as love. Inventory as affection. Marge wrote the lie in her mother's handwriting.

TASK 412C — APPLY HISTORIC ROUTE DECAL — 0.75 HRS
The discontinued roll. Adhesive yellowed. She peeled the backing, aligned the white arrows, smoothed the brown field with a squeegee. Bubbles trapped. She worked them to the edge with her thumb. The decal clung, stubborn as a grudge. The blank went on the sawhorse to cure. Three down. One more today if the adhesive held in the heat. Four signs. One hundred sixty dollars. Eight signs left for the month. Rent was four hundred. Electric ninety. Phone twenty. Food — she'd figure food.

Her phone rang. The shop landline, cord stretched across the bay to the folding table. "Road Department."
"Marge? It's Mom. I sent the check. Forty dollars. For the knee thing. You'll deposit it today?"
"Today's good, Mom."
"And the electric? You said — "
"Paid. Yesterday. Routine maintenance."
"Good. Good. You eat lunch?"
"Working on it." She hung up. The check. Forty dollars. Not a decal payment. Her mother's social security, divided. Logistics as love.

TASK 412D — INVOICE TOWNSHIP — 0.25 HRS
She filled the carbon triplicate. ROUTINE MAINTENANCE — SIGN REPLACEMENT MILE 9.3 SB. CREW: M. KETTERING. HRS: 1.5. RATE: $12.50. TOTAL: $18.75. White copy for the trustees. Yellow for the file. Pink for her. The carbon paper smeared. She pressed harder. The lie transferred clean through all three sheets. The trustees would sign. The historical society would pay cash. The benchmarks would stay buried under brown paint and federal specs. Henry Kettering's brass caps, sleeping in bedrock, waiting for satellites to fail.

She tore the pink copy, folded it, slipped it into her jeans pocket. The rent envelope sat on the passenger seat of her Duster, addressed, stamped. Four hundred dollars. She had one hundred sixty in the glovebox. The forty from her mother. Two hundred. Two hundred short. Eight signs at forty each. Three hundred twenty. She'd be twelve over. If the adhesive held. If the trustees didn't audit. If the historical society's grant didn't run dry.

She peeled the next decal. Brown over brown. The squeegee scraped. A bubble resisted, then surrendered.
