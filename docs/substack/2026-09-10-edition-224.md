## 📖 Today's Bit

### The Biofilm Docket

Dr. Kovic
The light catches the plaque differently today. Not the usual chalky smear on the lingual surfaces of the mandibular anteriors, but a geometric progression. Rows. Columns. A tiny, perfect grid blooming white against the enamel of tooth nineteen. I tap the scaler against the tray. *Tink. Tink.* "Open wider, Mr. Vance." He gurgles around the suction tip. I lean in, loupe magnification cranked to 4x. It’s a calendar. October. The 14th is circled in a biofilm ring so precise it looks laser-etched. "You’ve got a hot date," I say. "Or a root canal follow-up. Hard to read the legend."

Mr. Vance
The taste is copper and mint and the distinct, metallic pressure of her fingers pinching my cheek retractor. I can’t speak. But I know what’s on nineteen. It’s not a date. It’s a deadline. The bacteria—*my* flora, my ancient tenants—have organized. They’ve unionized. They spelled out "COURT 9 AM" in streptococcus mutans script because they know I’ll forget. They know the lawyer sends texts I delete. They know the alimony check bounces if I don’t deposit the cash advance by noon. Dr. Kovic thinks it’s cute. She thinks it’s plaque. She doesn't know the bugs are the only thing in this city keeping time for me.

Officer Miller
I stand by the operatory door, radio squelching low. "Kovic. Vance." I check the clipboard. The warrant is specific: *Erase the biofilm notation on Tooth 19. Prevent subject from attending Superior Court Docket 4421.* Contempt of court, failure to appear, the usual. The DA’s office calls it "evidence tampering via microbial vector." I call it a Tuesday. "Doc," I say, tapping the frame. "The scaler. The ultrasonic. Hit the distal buccal. Scrub the calendar." Kovic doesn't look up. She’s tracing the '9' with a probe. "He needs that tooth cleaned, Miller. Not politically sanitized." "It's a court order. The bugs are contraband."

Dr. Kovic
Miller’s hand rests on his holster. The city pays me $40k a year in "Forensic Dental Compliance" grants to keep the indigent bite-wing records straight. Vance pays me nothing but nausea and canceled appointments. But I looked at the grid. The bacteria didn't just write "Court." They wrote "Court -> Bus 14 -> Win." The arrow is a flagellum tail. The 'Win' is a cluster of cocci. They have hope, Miller. *Streptococcus sanguinis* has more institutional faith than the Public Defender's office. I pick up the ultrasonic scaler. The tip screams. "Hold still, Mr. Vance." I aim the vibrating tip at the 'W' in Win. The water spray aerosolizes the mint, the copper, the calendar. "Oops," I say. "Hand slipped. Calculus was tenacious." The grid dissolves into pink foam. "Reschedule," I tell the suction hose. "Tell the bugs... tell them November looks open."

Mr. Vance
The screaming stops. The vacuum swallows the schedule. She wiped it. She wiped the only reminder that worked. I feel the smooth, terrified clean of tooth nineteen with my tongue. A vacuum. I sit up, spitting pink into the cuspidor. "Doc," I say, voice raw. "You erased the 14th." She peels off her gloves, snap. "Insurance covers a prophy every six months, Vance. Next opening is November 3rd." She writes it on a paper card. Hands it to me. "Don't lose this one. Paper doesn't brush off." Miller grunts by the door. "November 3rd," he repeats. "Docket's continued anyway. Judge got the flu." I take the card. The ink is wet. I fold it, put it in my wallet next to a receipt for a burrito I ate three days ago. "Thanks, Doc," I say. "You're a prince." "Don't mention it," she says, not looking at me. "Floss. Or the next calendar grows on your tonsils."
