## 📖 Today's Bit

### The Grudge Hole

You shoulda seen Marta's face when the foreman tried to shovel the first apology into it. We're standing on 14th and Cedar, steam coming off the fresh asphalt, and this pothole — this *thing* — has been eating the neighborhood's beefs for three weeks. Old Mrs. Kowalski from 14B tossed in her thing about the landlord skipping boiler repairs. The hole took it, made this satisfied *glorp* sound, and the landlord showed up next morning with a new furnace and a face like a slapped ass.

So we're all getting paid overtime to feed it. Union rep negotiated hazard pay for "emotional sanitation work." Tommy from the roller brings his thing about his ex-wife keeping the good patio furniture. *Glorp.* Gone. The crew from Parks brings their thing about the sanitation crew never clearing the basketball courts. *Glorp.* Courts get cleared next Tuesday.

Then the foreman — Diaz, guy who pronounces "asphalt" like it's got three syllables — he pulls out a folded piece of notebook paper. Clears his throat. "On behalf of Marta Reyes, who regrets the incident regarding the borrowed ladder and the damaged gutter, and wishes to restore harmonious workplace relations."

Marta's standing there with her tamper, boots gray with dust, and she doesn't move. Doesn't blink. The hole *waits*. I swear to God, the steam changes pitch.

"I didn't write that," she says. Voice flat. "And I didn't say it."

Diaz sighs, the long-suffering kind. "Marta, the ladder. The gutter. Three hundred dollars of aluminum. You signed the incident report."

"I signed the report *you* wrote. I didn't borrow the ladder. Your nephew borrowed the ladder. He cracked the gutter. You made me sign because his insurance would've spiked."

The crew goes quiet. The roller idles. Even the hole seems to hold its breath.

Diaz's jaw works. "The city requires resolution. The hole requires resolution. You want to explain to the commissioner why 14th Street stays cracked because you're stubborn?"

Marta spits on the ground. "Fill it with gravel. Like a normal pothole."

"We can't. Ordinance 47-C. 'Anomalous subsurface features demonstrating restorative social function shall be maintained until dormant.' I didn't write the ordinance."

"Then let it stay open. Let people drive around it. Let them be mad."

"People *are* mad. That's the point. The hole eats the mad. You're mad. Feed it."

She stares at him. The tamper feels heavy in my hands just watching. Then she reaches into her vest pocket, pulls out her own crumpled receipt — hardware store, ladder rental, dated the Tuesday Diaz's nephew was on light duty.

"I'm not sorry," she says, dropping it in.

The hole doesn't *glorp*. It *coughs*. Spits the receipt back out, wet and smoking, right onto Diaz's boot.

Marta picks up her tamper. "Gravel's in the truck. Someone help me."

Nobody moves for a second. Then Tommy kills the roller. "I'll grab the rake."

The hole never took another thing. City filled it with cold patch two weeks later. Mrs. Kowalski's landlord raised the rent anyway. But Marta still works the crew, and Diaz's nephew still brings coffee every morning — black, two sugars, exactly how she takes it — and nobody talks about the receipt.

You ask me if it was worth it? Worth what? She's got her pension vesting in eighteen months. The ladder's still cracked. And sometimes, when the rain hits just right, you can see the outline of that pothole under the patch, dark as a bruise, waiting.
