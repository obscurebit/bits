# The Attendance Slip

Mrs. Gable's hydrangea died on a Tuesday, which meant the Attendance Slip on her kitchen counter turned from pale blue to the color of dried blood by Wednesday noon. I knew because I was the one who came to collect it.

"Mrs. Gable," I said, standing in her doorway with my clipboard. The air in the hallway smelled of boiled cabbage and coal dust. "The ordinance requires a signature. Or a payment. Or an apology. The Slip doesn't distinguish."

She peered over her spectacles, hands folded in her apron. "It was a hydrangea, Mr. Vane. A plant. I watered it. I talked to it. It died anyway. What does the Slip want me to apologize *for*?"

"Neglect," I read from the form, ticking the box. *[ ] Adequate [ ] Diminished [X] Requires Intervention.* "Or insufficient vitality rubric scores. Three weeks 'listless' triggers the transfer clause. You know the ordinance."

"I know the ordinance is a bastard written by men who think moss has feelings," she snapped. "And I know you, Arthur Vane. You used to bring me cuttings from the conservatory. The *Sphagnum* with the silver tips. You sang to them."

My face heated. The singing. That was the private skill. Low, off-key lullabies in the service corridor behind the display beds, while the misting headers hissed and the floor drains gulped. I'd taped a hand-drawn calendar of 'rest days' for each genotype beside the grease-pencil clipboard. *Genotype 4: Rest Day — Sing 'Down in the Valley'.* *Genotype 7: Rest Day — Hum Brahms.* The supervisor pretended not to see the 'Attitude' column I filled in: *Sullen. Pouting. Expectant.*

"They respond to vibration," I said stiffly. "It stimulates cytoplasmic streaming. It's in the municipal guidelines. Page twelve."

"Page twelve," she mimicked. "And the hydrangea? Did it respond to your vibration, Arthur? Or did it just die because it was old and the gas fumes from the stove got to it?"

The Slip on the counter darkened further, edging toward black. A death warrant for a household. If it went black, the bailiffs came for the furniture. For the teacups. For Mrs. Gable herself, eventually — transferred to the Home for the Botanically Negligent.

"I could sign," she said softly. "Say I was sorry. Say I'll do better. The Slip takes apologies same as signatures, you said."

"It does."

"Then I'll sign. And you'll take it. And the hydrangea stays dead, and I stay here, and the ordinance is satisfied." She held out a trembling hand for the pen.

I looked at the Slip. At the blackening edge. At the carbon copy beneath, waiting for my countersignature. *Dispatcher, Sector 4. A. Vane.*

My fingers found the grease pencil in my pocket. The one I used for the vitality rubric. *Color swatch: 4B. Moisture: 12g. Attitude: [ ] Adequate [X] Diminished [ ] Requires Intervention.*

I could alter the rubric. One stroke. Change the hydrangea's score from 'listless' to 'dormant.' Buy her three more months. The supervisor pretended not to see a lot of things.

But the transfer request for Genotype 4 — the silver-tipped *Sphagnum* — sat on my desk downtown. Three weeks 'listless.' The ordinance demanded I file it. Everyone knew 'transfer' meant the incinerator.

Mrs. Gable waited. The cabbage smell thickened. A tram rattled past outside, shaking the windowpanes.

"Mr. Vane," she said. "You're trembling. Is the form wrong?"

I looked at the hydrangea's dry stems in the chipped pottery on the windowsill. Thought of the moss trays in the service corridor, gulping mist, waiting for a lullaby only I knew they needed. Thought of the calendar I'd drawn, the rest days I'd assigned, the care I'd coded into bureaucracy because bureaucracy was the only language that protected anything.

"No," I said. "The form is correct."

I handed her the pen. She signed. *E. Gable.* A spidery scrawl. The Slip didn't lighten. It didn't darken further. It simply *was* — a gray rectangle of paper, indifferent, satisfied.

I countersigned. *A. Vane. Dispatcher. Sector 4.*

"Thank you, Arthur," she said. "For the cuttings. For the songs. Even if the guidelines say vibration stimulates cytoplasmic streaming."

I nodded, clutching the clipboard. "Page twelve," I said. "Municipal guidelines."

Outside, the tram rattled past again. I walked back to the conservatory through the damp November dusk, the carbon copy warm against my palm, and did not sing.
