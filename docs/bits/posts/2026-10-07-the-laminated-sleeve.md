# The Laminated Sleeve

I went to pull a pallet of tile adhesive from Bin 402 and found a corsage box sitting on the shrink-wrap. White cardboard, gold foil trim, the kind florists use for wristlets at prom. The manifest on the laminated sleeve said Bin 402 was empty. The sleeve itself — clouded plastic, edges frayed from thirty years of scanner friction — had been rewritten so many times the marker ink had bled into a single gray smudge. Block print for the 1987 receiving clerk. Cursive for the night-shift counter who took over in '91. Shaky initials, J.M., from the temp who lasted three weeks in '94. My own block print from last month, 402: EMPTY, written in the same black Sharpie everyone shares.

The corsage box wasn't on any pick list. Not in the WMS. Not in the paper backup binder the cage office keeps welded to Column 14, where the only window looks into a rack aisle and the AC vent drips on the shared keyboard. I picked it up. Light. Empty. A ceremonial support object, the district manager would call it if he ever came down from his climate-controlled office on the mezzanine. He doesn't. He reads the cycle count summaries I email him every Friday at 4:55 p.m., right before the unwritten rule kicks in: counts pause when he visits, discrepancies get resolved by moving pallets to unscanned overflow zones.

I took the box to the cage. Rosa, the temp agency clerk, was eating a microwaved tamale at the desk. She knew which forklift operators had revoked certifications but still got scheduled. She knew the district manager's assistant padded the overtime logs. She knew the corsage box had been in Bin 402 since before she started in '98, because the previous counter, a man named Dale who quit to sell insurance, told her over cigarettes behind the loading dock: *Don't delete it. Whatever you do, don't delete the line.*

"Why not?" I asked her now.

She shrugged, wiping tamale grease on a paper towel. "Dale said the box holds a spot. Not inventory. A spot. As long as it's there, the bin doesn't get reassigned. Soon as it's gone, someone puts fast-moving SKUs in 402. Pick rates go up. Someone gets a bonus. The box stays, the bin stays empty, nobody gets shit but the count stays clean."

"Clean," I said.

"Accurate," she corrected. "There's a difference."

I stood there holding a corsage box that had survived three district managers, two WMS migrations, and a roof collapse in '03 that flooded Aisle D but missed Column 14 by six feet. The laminate sleeve on Bin 402 had clouded further since I last looked. My own handwriting was already fading into the gray.

I put the box back on the pallet of tile adhesive. Scanned the bin. EMPTY. The gun beeped green.

Next week the district manager's assistant came down with a clipboard. New hire. Khakis, polo, the look of a man who'd never broken a pallet jack's steering wheel. He scanned Bin 402. Frowned. Scanned again.

"System says empty," he said. "But there's product here."

"Tile adhesive," I said. "Overflow from 401. Damaged wrap."

He nodded, made a note. "I'll flag it for recount."

He didn't ask about the corsage box tucked behind the pallet, gold foil catching the one stubborn shaft of daylight that fights the bad fixture. He didn't ask why the laminate sleeve has four layers of handwriting for a bin that's been officially empty since 1987.

The rule changed last month. Corporate mandated: all bins must reflect physical reality. No ghost locations. No ceremonial holds. The district manager signed off. The assistant scheduled the purge for Friday.

Rosa texted me from the cage office: *They're coming for 402. 2 p.m.*

I looked at the corsage box. At the laminate sleeve. At the Sharpie in my pocket.

At 1:55, I scanned Bin 402. The gun beeped red. EXCEPTION: LOCATION NOT FOUND.

I wrote on the sleeve in fresh black ink, over the gray smudge, over Dale's shaky initials, over my own fading block print: **402: CORSAGE BOX — DO NOT DELETE.**

The assistant scanned it at 2:03. His gun beeped green. He checked his clipboard. Nodded. "Matches."

Rosa ate her tamale. The AC vent dripped. The district manager's bonus cleared payroll on the 15th.

I still have the Sharpie.
