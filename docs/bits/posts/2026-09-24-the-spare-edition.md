# The Price of Row F

The Model 440s arrived in September: flip-down, powder-coated, 350-lb rating, proprietary firmware. Installers left a laminated guide and a 1-800 number. The feature wasn't in the brochure. It dropped in the v3.1 patch: "Enhanced Occupancy Logic: Dynamic Allocation Based on Relational Integrity Metrics." Marketing called it Community-First Seating. The union called it a grievance. I called it a bandwidth issue.

The logic scanned linked accounts and the venue app's message history. "Sorry" weighted positive. "Whatever" weighted negative. "It's fine" voided the warranty. High-integrity parties got center orchestra. Low-integrity got the wings, or the accessible row—an ADA nightmare.

Opening night, my brother Marcus appeared twenty minutes to curtain. Two tickets via a corporate code I'd told him not to use. Puffer vest indoors, cracked iPhone 8.

"Keith. Row G."

"System assigns seats, Marcus. I don't override."

"Since when?"

"Since the update. The memo you didn't read."

"We're brothers. Same family plan. Metric should be maxed."

"Did you apologize?"

"For what?"

"The truck. The transmission. Three grand I fronted."

"Two years ago. I Venmo-requested half the tires last month. You declined."

"Passive-aggressive."

"Four hundred dollars for tires *I* bought."

A family of four blew past, mother scanning, father herding twins toward Row C, center. Pristine integrity. Probably wrote thank-you notes to the babysitter.

Marcus exhaled. "I'm sorry, Keith. For the truck. For not paying you back. For being a drain. Happy? Can we sit?"

Tablet chimed. *Allocation Updated: Party of 2 — Row J, Seats 14-15 (Side Orchestra, Partial View).*

"Side orchestra. Partial view. 'Accepted but unverified' tier. Full restitution unlocks Row F. Documented repayment plan gets Row C. You want center? Auto-pay on the Venmo. Three hundred a month. System verifies in real time."

He stared. House lights dimmed. Overture started tinny through lobby speakers.

"Fine. Auto-pay. But memo line: 'Blackmail.'"

"Twenty-character limit. Try 'Reconciliation.'"

He tapped. Tablet chimed. *Allocation Updated: Party of 2 — Row F, Seats 8-9 (Center Orchestra).*

"Enjoy the show. No refunds for buyer's remorse. Terms of Service 4.2."

He walked off, shoulders tight. I checked the dashboard. Row J, 14-15 flashed green: *Vacant — Reallocated to Waitlist Party (Integrity Score: 94th Percentile).*

A young couple materialized from the bar, took the seats Marcus had held for thirty seconds. Looked happy. Probably apologized for everything. Probably meant it.

I closed the tablet. *A Christmas Carol* with aerial silks and a non-union Scrooge. Four times seen, forty to go. The chairs knew more about my family than my therapist. Better uptime, too.

Next season: v4.0, "Empathy-Driven Concession Pricing." Beta tested dynamic popcorn costs based on vocal stress analysis at intermission. I'd already requested a box office transfer. Less walking. Better wifi. The chairs there didn't judge. They just took your money.
