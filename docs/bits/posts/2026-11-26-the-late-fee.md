# The Late Fee

You arrive at the nutrition center's rear dock seventeen minutes past the shift change. The yellow stripe on the concrete has faded to the color of old mustard, but the rule still holds: human food bank left, colony substrate right. The inspector's clipboard clicks against the metal railing.

Check Box 1: Bin sealed. Initialed M.R. (Margot Reyes, your sister, who mistook competence for consent when she volunteered you for Gold Tier certification without asking.)

Check Box 2: Moisture log current. Initialed M.R. (She initialed Tuesday through Saturday. Sunday is blank. Monday is blank. Today is Wednesday.)

Check Box 3: No contamination visible. Initialed — (The inspector's pen hovers. She is your cousin's ex-wife. She knows about the mortgage modification. She knows about the car payment.)

The mycelium colony in Bin 4 fruited overnight. White tendrils press against the lid's ventilation holes, smelling of rain on hot asphalt and something older, mineral. They honored their moisture quota. The log says you did too.

Check Box 4: Hydration schedule maintained. Initialed — (Your hand trembles. The pen leaves a ink blob beside the box.)

"Margot said you'd handle it," the inspector says. Her name is Dina. She once lent you forty dollars for a tire repair. You paid her back in three installments. She has never forgotten the schedule.

"Margot says a lot of things," you say.

Check Box 5: Household compliance verified. Initialed — (The colony pulses. A faint bioluminescence traces the ventilation holes — Morse code for *liar*, *liar*, *liar*, if you knew the pattern. The colonies map deception through root networks. Gold Tier households get priority soil returns. Your tomatoes last summer were the envy of the block.)

"You're late," Dina says. "The rule changed Tuesday. Nobody explained it. Something about consensus requiring presence."

"Consensus," you repeat. "Right."

Check Box 6: Inspector discretion exercised. Initialed D.V. (She checks it. She checks it slow. Her fingernails are bitten to the quick.)

"Your sister's bins passed," Dina says. "Blocks 3 and 7. She reported full hydration. The colonies disagree."

"Blocks 3 and 7," you say. "The ones with the new tenants."

"Tenants don't mist," Dina says. "Owners do. Certification follows the deed."

The deed is in your name. The mortgage modification put it there. Margot lives in the back unit. She waters her actual plants — succulents, hardy, forgiving — and forgets the bins. You mist the bins. You check the logs. You initial the boxes she misses.

Check Box 7: Discrepancy noted. Initialed — (You don't initial. You wait.)

The colony in Bin 4 shudders. A spore cloud puffs through the ventilation holes, fine as talc, settling on Dina's clipboard. She sneezes. The sound echoes off the loading dock walls.

"Bless you," you say.

"Don't," she says. "It's not a blessing. It's data."

Check Box 8: Spore event recorded. Initialed D.V. (She writes it. She writes *household dispute — refer to mediation* in the margin. Her handwriting slopes left, tired.)

"You could fail us," you say. "Lose the certification. Waste fees triple. Soil returns go to waitlist."

"Could," Dina agrees. "Won't. Your tomatoes fed six families last August. The colony remembers. They voted."

"Colonies don't vote."

"Everything votes," Dina says. "The rule nobody can explain: consensus includes the non-verbal. The inspector records. The colony decides. I'm just the pencil."

She tears the top sheet. The carbon copy stays. She hands you the original.

"Gold Tier renewed," she says. "Conditional. You initial the real log. Every day. Sunday too. Monday too. I check weekly. You miss one — "

"I won't miss."

"Your sister will."

"Margot's not my problem."

Dina laughs. It sounds like dry leaves. "That's the new rule. You initial. She benefits. The colony watches. Everyone pretends it's fair."

She walks toward the front entrance, clipboard tucked against her hip. The yellow stripe glows faintly under the security lights — fresh paint, applied last night while you slept.

Check Box 9: New rule accepted. Initialed — (You sign. The ink dries fast. The colony in Bin 4 goes dark, satisfied.)

You pull your keys. The car starts on the second try. Margot's light is on in the back unit. Through the window: she's watering a jade plant, careful drops, perfect aim. She waves.

You drive past the community garden, past the compost rows where last year's Gold Tier soil grows kale the size of dinner plates. The colonies mapped your deception. They voted anyway.

You keep driving.
