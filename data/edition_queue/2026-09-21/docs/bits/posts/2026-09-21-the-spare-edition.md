# The Wet Stitches

The stitching on the cardigan only existed while wet. Marta had seen it twice now: pale blue thread forming block letters across the lower back, visible for the seven minutes between the washer's final rinse and the dryer's first blast of heat. *HE'S NOT DEAD. HE'S WAITING.*

She folded the garment dry, the message gone, the wool soft and unmarked. The receipt said Elena Varga. Pickup 4:15. It was 4:08.

Marta checked the lint trap, wiped the folding table, stared at the clock. The laundromat smelled of detergent and the particular exhaustion of Tuesday afternoons. Outside, rain streaked the plate-glass windows.

The door chimed. A girl, maybe sixteen, hood up, headphones around her neck. She moved straight to the counter without looking at the machines.

"My mom's sweater. Elena Varga."

Marta's fingers found the ticket. "Four dollars even."

The girl paid with a crumpled five, didn't wait for change. "She's parking. Bad knee."

Marta nodded, retrieved the cardigan from the shelf. The wool felt ordinary in her hands. She placed it in a clear bag, folded the top.

"She okay? Your mom."

The girl paused, hand on the bag. "She's fine. Why?"

"No reason. Take care."

The girl stepped outside, leaving the bag while she helped her mother park. Two minutes later, a silver Honda pulled up. Elena Varga emerged, favoring her left leg, and came inside shaking an umbrella.

"Your daughter's paid. Cardigan's ready."

Elena took the bag; Marta returned the extra dollar to her. Her fingers brushed the wool through the plastic. "Thank you, Marta. You're a lifesaver."

"Sure."

Elena paused at the door. "She doesn't know. My daughter. About her father."

Marta kept folding a stranger's socks. "Okay."

"He wrote to us. For three years. Letters I never gave her. I told her he died in the accident." Elena's voice was flat. "The cardigan was his. She thinks I'm wearing it for sentiment."

Marta said nothing.

"I should tell her. Today. Before she finds the letters." Elena looked at her hands. "But if I do, she'll hate me for the lying. If I don't, she'll hate me for the silence. There's no version where I'm not the villain."

The rain hammered the windows. Somewhere, a dryer buzzed.

Marta stopped folding. She looked at Elena—really looked. The worn coat. The careful way she held her purse. The gray at her temples.

"You're not the villain," Marta said. "You're the one who stayed."

Elena's breath caught. She nodded once, hard, and left.

Marta returned to the socks. The clock read 4:23. The laundromat hummed on, indifferent, clean.
