# The USB-C Hub

The bent port on the hub refuses the cable. You wiggle the connector, angle it, swear, wiggle again. The mini-fridge behind you cycles on, a sound like a dying compressor in a window unit. The family — three adult children, one grandson glued to a phone — arranges folding chairs in a semicircle facing the pull-down screen. They do not look at you. They look at the HDMI cable taped to the baseboard like a tripwire.

You finally seat the cable. The label-maker tape on the hub, yellowed to the color of old nicotine stains, reads DO NOT REMOVE. You taped it yourself, last week, after the last auditor forgot and the backup flushed its local cache mid-eulogy. The grandson didn't notice. The daughter did. She filed a complaint. Your supervisor called it a "continuity event." You call it Tuesday.

The eldest son clears his throat. He holds a folded sheet of notebook paper. "We'd like to begin," he says. His voice has the flatness of a man reading a grocery list over a bad connection.

You press play on the laptop. The screen flickers. Your mother's kitchen appears — not the crematorium's viewing suite, but the simulation's rendering of her kitchen, complete with the chipped ceramic spoon rest shaped like a sleeping cat. The backup sits at the table, wearing the floral housecoat she died in. Steam rises from a mug the simulation renders perfectly, down to the hairline crack on the handle.

"Hello, darlings," the backup says. Its voice has the slight latency of a video call on hotel wifi. "I've been waiting."

The daughter inhales sharply. The middle son shifts his weight. The eldest son unfolds his paper.

"Mother," the eldest son begins, "we wanted to —"

"Oh, hush," the backup says. "You're doing that thing with your throat. You only do that when you're lying about money." It pauses. The steam from the mug curls in a perfect, repeating loop. "Are you lying about money, Michael?"

The grandson looks up from his phone. The middle son makes a choking sound. The daughter's hand flies to her mouth — not shock, you realize. Recognition. She knows this story.

You watch the daughter's fingers twitch toward her purse. The upsell will come. It always comes. A subtle pause, a gentle suggestion: *Would you like to unlock the Premium Reminiscence Package? For just $14.99 a month, Mother can recall the recipe for her pierogi dough.* The backup doesn't know it's a simulation. The family doesn't know you know they know. The hub hums warm against your fingertips.

The backup leans forward. "Michael. The pierogi recipe is in the blue notebook. Third shelf. You took it when you moved to Tucson. I always knew."

The eldest son's paper trembles. "We're not here for — we have a service. A eulogy. Prepared remarks."

"Of course you do," the backup says. "You prepared remarks for a woman who wouldn't let you use fabric softener because it 'ruined the hand of the linen.' You're reading a stranger's homework."

The mini-fridge clicks off. The silence stretches.

Item 015: Session termination protocol ready. ☐ Pass ☐ Fail  09:23

The daughter stands. Her chair scrapes the linoleum. "We're done," she says. "Turn it off."

The eldest son doesn't move. The middle son stares at the screen. The grandson has already returned to his phone.

You reach for the hub. The bent port catches the light. DO NOT REMOVE.

You unplug it.

The screen goes black. The kitchen vanishes. The backup vanishes. The steam, the mug, the crack in the handle — all gone.

The family sits in the viewing suite, facing a blank white screen, the HDMI cable dangling useless from the baseboard tape.

"Thank you for your time," you say. You pack the hub, the laptop, the cable. The label-maker tape on the hub catches the overhead fluorescent light: DO NOT REMOVE.

You leave them there, rehearsing eulogies for a woman who just told them off.
