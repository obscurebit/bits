# The Flat Ninth

I kept a spiral notebook for the voices. Not names — just addresses, dates, and the intervals they hid in. 442 Sycamore, October 1998: a minor third in the tenor register that resolved to a major sixth if you struck the key twice. A woman's laugh, low and surprised, like she'd just remembered a joke she wasn't supposed to tell.

Mr. Velez called me at six a.m. on a Saturday. His daughter, Elena, sixteen, Chopin's Third Ballade in two weeks. He offered double my rate if I could make the Baldwin "disappear" — his word. He wanted the piano to become invisible, just music.

I arrived before dawn. The house smelled of floor wax and old coffee. Elena sat at the keyboard in a bathrobe, running scales with the mechanical precision of someone who'd been taught to hate mistakes. Her father hovered in the doorway, checkbook ready.

The Baldwin was a 1922 Model M, soundboard cracked along the grain. Standard tuning would take three hours. "Perfect" tuning — the kind that holds through humidity shifts and heavy hands — takes six. I quoted him the real price. He wrote the check without looking at the amount.

Around hour four, I found it. A-flat above middle C, tuned twelve cents sharp. Not drift. Deliberate. Someone had turned the pin with a hammer, not a lever. I corrected it, and the room changed. Not metaphorically. The air pressure shifted. Dust motes stalled.

A voice spoke from the A-flat. *Not that one. The next one.*

I struck the A-natural. *Closer. But you're rushing the trill.*

Elena stopped playing. Her father's pen froze mid-signature.

"Who's there?" Velez asked the empty room.

*Your daughter,* the voice said. *She practices at midnight. She plays the coda wrong on purpose. She wants you to hear the mistake so you'll stop paying for lessons.*

Elena's face went the color of the piano's ivory. Velez looked at me, then at her, then at the check on the music rack.

"You heard that," he said. Not a question.

"I hear a lot of things," I said. "Pianos talk. Mostly they complain."

*Tell him about the scholarship,* the A-flat insisted. *Tell him you turned it down.*

"I didn't — " Elena started.

*You did. The Juilliard letter. In the cookie tin. Behind the flour.*

Velez crossed the room in three strides. The tin sat on the highest shelf. He needed a step stool. I heard the paper tear before he read it.

The voice never spoke again. Not in that piano, not in any piano. I finished the tuning. The Baldwin sang like a bell choir. Elena played the Ballade at the recital. She won the regional competition. She never applied to conservatory.

Velez paid me the doubled rate plus a tip. He also gave me the spiral notebook I'd left on the bench. I'd never brought a notebook to that job.

Inside, in handwriting that wasn't mine: *442 Sycamore. The tuner hears. He always hears. Don't tell him about the others.*

I still have the notebook. The last entry is dated yesterday. My address. My piano. Middle C, five cents flat. *You're next.*
