# The Super's Daughter's Ledger

**Vestibule Chalk Wall — Tuesday**
3PKG 4B MRS K ✓
1FRAG 2A MR V → FLOOR
HOOK TIP BENT 07:14
R REQ HOLD 5C → PRIORITY
DAD ASLEEP RADIATOR 3 TAPS

**Package Room**
Mara, nineteen, smelled machine oil and wet cardboard before she opened her eyes. The dumbwaiter shaft breathed cold behind the mailboxes. Her father's brass parcel hook hung from a nail, its tip bent like a question mark from last winter when Mrs. Kowalski's sewing machine crate slipped. Mara used the bend to snag high shelves. She used the straight shaft to tap Morse on radiator pipes: *Dad asleep. Three taps. Come back at noon.*

The ledger sat open on the apple crate desk. Carbon paper duplicated every signature. Mara knew the delivery drivers' handwriting better than her own: Henderson's cramped block letters, O'Leary's looping cursive, the new boy from Acme who printed like a schoolboy. She knew the tenants' lies. Mr. Vane in 2A always claimed he never signed for the fragile box. He signed. She watched him sign. She watched him smile at her father, then at her, the smile different each time.

**Laundry Room — 09:47**
Rosa Vane stood at the wringer, wrists raw from lye soap. She didn't look at Mara. "Tell your father the rent's Friday. Tell him I'll have it."

"Friday," Mara said. She clipped the brass hook to her belt loop. The bent tip caught the denim.

"And the package. The one from the agency. You held it."

"Held it?"

"Don't." Rosa's fingers found a wet sheet, twisted. "We were friends. Before. You know what it means."

Mara knew. The agency envelope. Thin. Official. The kind that changed a family's address. Rosa had brought Mara licorice whips from the penny candy jar at Goldstein's. Mara had brought Rosa the key to the roof door so she could smoke away from her mother's cough. That was before Rosa's father lost the warehouse job. Before Rosa started taking in washing. Before Mara's father made her superintendent's daughter, which meant keys, which meant knowledge, which meant power she hadn't asked for.

**Vestibule Chalk Wall — 10:02**
ROSA V 2A → ROOF KEY RETURNED
AGENCY ENV 5C HOLD → PRIORITY
HOOK TAP RADIATOR 2 TAPS = DAD WOKE

**Mailbox Alley — 10:15**
The new Acme driver stood at the counter, uniform crisp, badge shiny. "Package for 5C. Registered. Signature required."

Mara didn't reach for the pen. She tapped the brass hook against the radiator pipe. *Two taps. Dad woke.*

The driver blinked. "Ma'am?"

"Leave it. I'll sign." She scribbled *M. KOWALSKI — AUTH* in the signature box. The carbon copy bled through. She slid the envelope behind the loose brick in the dumbwaiter shaft, the one only she and her father knew about.

**Roof Access — 11:30**
Rosa waited by the tar-paper hatch, a basket of damp sheets at her feet. Wind flattened her hair. "You have it."

"I have it."

"Give it to me."

Mara felt the brass hook warm against her hip. The bent tip. The straight shaft. The Morse code she'd taught herself from a library book her father threw out. *Need. Not authorization.* The hook granted access according to need. Her father's rule. Her rule now.

"Friday," Mara said. "You get the envelope Friday. Rent day."

Rosa's face closed. "You're keeping it. As leverage."

"I'm keeping it safe. There's a difference."

"No. There isn't."

**Vestibule Chalk Wall — 11:59**
5C ENV → ROOF HATCH BRICK
ROSA V 2A → WAITING
RULE: NEED BEATS AUTH
NEW RULE: WAIT BEATS NEED

**Package Room — Noon**
Her father woke coughing, the radiator clanking steam. He reached for the brass hook, found the nail empty. Found Mara at the desk, carbon paper staining her fingertips.

"Mara."

"Dad."

"Where's the 5C registered?"

"Held. Priority."

He studied her. The superintendent's eyes, watery and sharp. He knew every tenant's rent date, every driver's route, every lie told at this counter. He didn't know about the roof key. Didn't know about the licorice whips. Didn't know the agency envelope was thin enough to slip behind a brick.

"Your call," he said. "You're the one here."

He sat on the apple crate. Closed his eyes. The radiator hissed.

Mara tapped the hook against the pipe. *Three taps. Dad asleep. Come back at noon.*

She waited. The chalk wall waited. The envelope waited behind the brick.

Rosa would come Friday. Rent day. The envelope would come out. Or it wouldn't.

Mara wrote on the wall in chalk, pressing hard: *FRIDAY 5C ENV → ROSA V IF RENT PAID. IF NOT → AGENCY RETURNS. RULE STANDS.*

The hook hung from her belt, bent tip catching light.
