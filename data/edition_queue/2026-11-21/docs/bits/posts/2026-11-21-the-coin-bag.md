# The Coin Bag

"You're short three dollars again," Marisol said, not looking up from her ledger. The pen scratched. "That's the third time this month."

You set the canvas bag on the metal table. The zipper's brass teeth caught the fluorescent light. "The meter on Maple ate quarters all morning. I wrote it up."

"Write-ups don't feed the armored car." She finally looked up. Her nails were painted a chipped coral, the same shade as the vending machine's fake cherry wrappers. "You know the rule. Shortages come from your pocket."

The rule. You'd carried the laminated card since 2012 — *Meter Maintenance Quick-Reference, Municipal Parking Authority, Rev. 3* — even after the app-based enforcement made it obsolete. The card lived in your wallet behind your license. You could still recite Section 4.2: *Vault discrepancies exceeding $2.00 require dual-signature verification before armored transport.*

Marisol didn't need the card. She'd been senior auditor since before the merger that killed the credit union printed on your bag. First Community Federal. The name faded to a ghost on gray canvas.

**Footnote 4.2a: Dual-signature verification requires both auditors present at vault opening. Exception: Route 7 construction zones, where single-signature protocol applies due to safety hazards.**

Route 7. Your route. The "dirty" one. Construction zones, broken meters, residents who threw bricks at enforcement vehicles. Marisol took Route 3 — tree-lined streets, working meters, retirees who waved from porches.

"It's three dollars," you said. "I'll cover it."

"You say that every time." She capped her pen. "But you're a temp. Temps don't cover. The system covers."

The system. The new app-based enforcement. Real-time sync. No more coin vaults, no more armored cars, no more canvas bags. Next month, the bag would be retired. You with it.

**Footnote 4.2b: Single-signature protocol authorized only when senior auditor cosigns weekly reconciliation. Temporary auditors lack cosign authority.**

You didn't have cosign authority. You'd never been told.

The vending machine hummed. 3:47 PM. The lights flickered — they always did, same time, same rhythm. A bag of discontinued cheese curls trembled in coil C4.

"Marisol," you said. "The Maple meter. The quarter sensor — it's the old mechanism. The 2012 manual says—"

"The manual's trash." She stood, gathering her ledger. "We use the app now. The app says you're short. The app doesn't lie."

You touched the bag's zipper pull. Cold brass. The credit union had merged into Regional Trust, then Regional Trust into National Bancorp, then National Bancorp into something called Unity Financial. The bag had outlived three institutions.

**Footnote 4.2c: In cases of systemic sensor failure affecting multiple meters on a single route, temporary auditor liability is waived pending engineering review.**

Maple. Oak. Elm. Three meters on Route 7 with the same quarter sensor. You'd written up all three. Marisol had signed the write-ups. She'd cosigned.

She knew.

She walked to the door, coral nails clicking on her phone screen. "Armored car's at the bay. Don't be late."

The door clicked shut.

You unzipped the bag. Coin rolled — quarters, dimes, nickels — spilling across the metal table. Three dollars in quarters. Twelve coins. You counted them twice.

The manual didn't lie. But you'd misunderstood. The waiver wasn't for you. It was for *her*. Senior auditor cosign triggered the waiver. She'd signed the write-ups knowing the waiver would apply, knowing the system would absorb the loss, knowing you'd pay anyway because you didn't know to claim it.

The temp pays. The system protects its own.

You swept the quarters into your palm. Walked to the vending machine. Coil C4. Cheese curls. $1.50. You fed in six quarters.

The bag trembled. The lights steadied.

Outside the windowless room, the armored car's engine growled. You zipped the canvas bag — lighter now, three dollars lighter — and carried it to the loading bay.

The driver didn't look up. "Count?"

"Count's good," you said.

He scanned the barcode. The handheld beeped. Green light.

"Sign here."

You signed. The pen scratched like Marisol's. The receipt printed warm and curling.

**Footnote 4.2d: All vault transfers final upon armored carrier signature. No adjustments permitted post-transfer.**

You tore the receipt. Folded it. Slipped it behind the laminated card in your wallet.

The bag would be retired next month. You might not.

But today, the count was good. The transfer final. The transaction complete.
