# Existence Verification, Q3

The stamp pad had gone crusty violet again. She stabbed the syringe needle through the dried surface, depressed the plunger, watched generic purple fluid bleed into the fibers. Three blocks to the art supply store. Four dollars. The Department of Revenue would not reimburse.

*Applicant 047 — mailbox leans left now. Same rusted hinge.*

The walk-up window hummed. Bulletproof glass distorted voices into a low register, everyone sounding like they'd swallowed gravel. The former check-cashing storefront still smelled of lemon cleaner, aggressive and recent. Someone had scrubbed the counter until the laminate shone. Nobody mentioned what happened here last month. The night deposit tray had been pried open. Or maybe someone just bled on the floor. In the Unmapped Properties annex, you learned not to ask.

*Applicant 012 — saw cat in window. Same tabby as 2023. Calico, left ear notched.*

Mara flipped the form. Back side: notarized letter from a neighbor. Photo of front door with today's newspaper. Current utility bill — gas, electric, water, any two. The newspaper requirement was her favorite piece of institutional theater. As if the date on a front page proved anything about the door behind it. As if the door proved anything about the building. As if the building existed on any map the city recognized.

*Applicant 033 — hands shake when signing. Arthritis? Fear? Both.*

"Next."

The voice through the glass: male, mid-forties, tired. He slid the packet under the tray. Gas bill. Electric bill. Notarized letter from Mrs. Chen upstairs — *I, Mei-Ling Chen, affirm residence of above-named at 404 1/2 Oak Street.* Photo: blue door, peeling paint, today's *Chronicle* folded in the storm door's metal grate. Headline: *Council Delays Vote on Flood Zone Remapping.*

Mara checked the gas bill against the master list. 404 1/2 Oak Street. Not on the tax rolls. Not on the zoning map. Not on the emergency services grid. The building existed in the same way a ghost exists: witnesses, habits, an address people spoke aloud.

*Applicant 033 — gas bill matches Q2. Electric bill matches. Mrs. Chen's handwriting consistent. Notary stamp current.*

She picked up the stamp. *VERIFIED — UNMAPPED PROPERTIES ANNEX — Q3 2024.* The purple ink came out uneven, patchy. She pressed harder.

*Applicant 033 — cat in window photo. Tabby, not calico. Different cat.*

Her pen hovered over the marginal annotation line.

The man's voice through the glass: "Everything in order?"

"One moment." She turned the photo toward the light. The tabby sat in the same window frame as the calico from last quarter. Same chipped sill. Same cracked pane. Different animal.

*Applicant 033 — new cat. Applicant claims same household.*

She wrote: *Cat changed. Noted.*

"Ma'am?"

The pen didn't move. The form didn't move. The stamp pad sat crusty violet under the syringe.

*Applicant 033 — if cat changed, what else? Address same. Bills same. Neighbor same. Notary same. Only the cat.*

"Ma'am, my break —"

"Your break is in twenty minutes." Her voice came out flat, professional. The glass ate the warmth. "Sign here. And here."

He signed. His hands didn't shake this time.

*Applicant 033 — signed without tremor. Verified. Stamped. Filed.*

She slid the packet back. The stamp made a wet *thock* on the receipt copy. *VERIFIED — UNMAPPED PROPERTIES ANNEX — Q3 2024.* Purple ink bled into the paper fibers.

"Next quarter," she said. "Same requirements. Newspaper. Bills. Letter. Photo."

He took the packet. "The cat's mine. My sister's cat died. I took hers."

Mara didn't look up. *Applicant 033 — volunteered cat explanation unprompted. Noted.*

"Next."

The window stayed empty. The lemon cleaner smell sharpened. She uncapped the syringe, drew more purple fluid, stabbed the pad again.

*Applicant 033 — Q3 verified. Fee: $17.50. Paid cash. No receipt requested.*

The money sat on the counter. She didn't touch it.
