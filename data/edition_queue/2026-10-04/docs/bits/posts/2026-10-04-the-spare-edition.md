# The Promise Lifeguard

The whistle tastes like chlorine and old pennies. I blew it three times. Standard code for *contaminant in the deep end*. The kid floating face-down wasn't drowning. He was dissolving. His skin going translucent, ribs showing like architecture. The water doesn't take cash anymore. Hasn't since the '98 Recession. It takes collateral. Futures. The deep end is twelve feet of liquid escrow.

—

I didn't authorize this. My little brother, Leo, seven years old, pink goggles, frog-kick like a wounded seal. He handed the water a promise: *I'll never yell at Mom again.* Stupid kid promise. The kind that evaporates by Tuesday. The water accepted it. Gave him a wristband woven from filament and fool's gold. Now the pool owns his Thursday afternoons forever. I need that promise back. I have braces to pay for. I have a shift at the car wash Friday.

—

The receipt printer in the guard shack sputtered. Thermal paper curling. *Transaction ID: 7734-P. Asset: Sibling Temperance (Perpetual). Status: LIQUIDATED.* I highlighted the error code with a Sharpie. Red ink on thermal. Void the transaction. Refund the asset. The pool hates that. The pool has a union rep. The pool files grievances through the Department of Submerged Equity. I've seen the forms. Triplicate. Watermarked.

—

The lifeguard — new hire, nose ring, reads *The Economist* on break — she won't meet my eyes. She keeps tapping her clipboard. *Policy 402: No Returns on Intangibles.* I know Policy 402. I wrote the margin notes in the 2016 revision. *Exception 402-C: Minor's Contract Voidable by Legal Guardian.* I am not his guardian. I am his sister. I have a notarized letter from Mom. Dated yesterday. Notarized by Mr. Henderson at the UPS Store. He smells like toner and regret.

—

The water rose to my ankles. The shallow end flooding the deck. Retaliation. The pool *does* that. It remembers. I saw it swallow a City Councilman's reelection campaign whole in '04. Just... drank the podium. The microphones kept broadcasting bubbles for weeks. I grabbed the void form. Wet fingers. Paper disintegrating. The pool whispered through the drains: *Interest accrues daily. Compound. Soul-adjacent.*

—

Leo screamed from the deep end. Not fear. *Boredom.* "I want my gummy vitamins back!" He'd promised those too. Tuesday's allotment. The water spat them out — three red sharks, expanded to the size of pool noodles, floating mocking. The lifeguard finally moved. Red rescue tube arcing. She didn't save him. She *served* him. Subpoena. *In re: Promise #7734-P. Hearing: Tomorrow. 0600 Hours. Bring Counsel.* The water receded. Leo stood shivering on the tiles, opaque again. My sneakers squelched.

—

I have counsel. My cousin Vinny. He handles traffic court and demonic possession. Same rate. He'll wear the seersucker suit. He'll argue *unconscionability*. The pool will laugh. But he doesn't need to win the argument. Mom's letter withdraws her consent. Dated yesterday, it precedes Leo's promise. Clause 402-C makes the clerk unwind the transaction. Leo gets his Thursdays back. I get my Friday shift. The gummy sharks go in the trash. The lifeguard gets a raise. The pool gets... nothing. For once. I sign the void form in ballpoint. Blue ink. The paper holds.
