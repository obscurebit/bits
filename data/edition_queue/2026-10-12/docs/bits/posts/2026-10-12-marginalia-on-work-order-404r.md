# Marginalia on Work Order 404-R

Box 1: Requestor — I wrote my own name. The form requires a department code; I used the old one for Print Shop Annex, dissolved 2002. Nobody checks. The spectrophotometer drifted in 2003. The budget line for calibration was zeroed out the same week the mayor's nephew got the vending machine concession. I still mix ink by weight. Linseed oil, gum arabic, the dented 1976 formula guide open to the swatch the city calls gold. It yellowed to that shade years ago. No current vendor matches it. The guide is the only standard left.

Box 2: Description of Defect — The flag batch shipping tomorrow varies Delta E 2.8. Within tolerance. The unwritten rule: anything under 3.0 ships. The mayor's office ordered five hundred. The warehouse lease renews in six weeks. I signed the release. Hernandez watched me sign. Hernandez always watches. He wants my shift lead stipend. He thinks I cheat the mix. I don't cheat. I remember. The guide remembers for me. The humidity in the mixing room warps the paper stock. The air holds the weight of every batch since the committee designed the flag in the seventies. They argued about the shade of blue for six meetings. The gold was an afterthought. A compromise. The sigil nobody noticed.

Box 3: Corrective Action Taken — I added three grams of cadmium yellow. The scale reads to a tenth of a gram. I lied on the log. I wrote "humidity adjustment." The log is the infrastructure. The cheat became the procedure. Ten years ago I did it to save a batch. Five years ago I did it because the scale drifted. Last year I did it because Hernandez was standing there with his clipboard. Today I did it because the tenant in 3B — the one who pays rent in folded twenties and complains about the hallway light flickering — called the mayor's hotline about a flag on the pole outside his window. He said the gold looked wrong. He is right. He is unbearable. The mayor's office called the shop. The shop foreperson called me. I am the foreperson. I called myself.

Box 4: Parts Replaced — None. The formula guide stays in the drawer. The drawer sticks. I have to kick it. The kick is part of the procedure now. The sigil works because the procedure works. The flag on the pole outside 3B will fly for a year. The gold will fade to the exact shade in the guide. The tenant will complain again. The mayor will order five hundred more. The warehouse lease will renew. Hernandez will get the stipend when I retire. I have rehearsed the confrontation where I tell him the truth. I have rehearsed it so often the words have no sound. The truth is: the flag is a container. The gold is the lid. The city is the leftovers. We ship the batch tomorrow. I will sign the release. I will kick the drawer. I will not retire.
